## Chapter 1: The Three-Generation Blueprint — Evolution and Myths

The Kremers family didn't find the laundry business — it found us.

My grandfather **Jerry** ran Speed Queen routes through Arkansas long before "passive income" was a buzzword. He taught me that clean clothes were just the surface — the real business was dependability.

Dad carried the torch with **Kremers Laundry Equipment Company**. He sold, repaired, and designed laundromats back when everything was still quarters and keys. He rebuilt Maytag 5-light top loaders, installed Dexters, and designed self-service layouts that still stand today.

Then came me — the third generation. I grew up in the back of a service truck. My first toolkit came before my first report card. I learned to lift machines before I learned to balance a checkbook.

### The Modern Approach

You don't just build a laundromat. You **architect** one — every lease, machine, and inch of tile is a decision that either compounds or collapses.

**The Three Pillars of the Kremers Way:**

1. **Foundation First** — Location and lease determine 80% of success
2. **Systems Over Hustle** — Design beats improvisation every time
3. **Numbers Don't Lie** — Track metrics religiously or fail slowly

### Debunking the Myths

**Myth #1: "Laundromats are passive income"**
Reality: They're SYSTEMATIZED income. Well-designed stores can run with minimal intervention, but someone is always watching the numbers.

**Myth #2: "Any location will work"**
Reality: Location is LAW. A bad location cannot be fixed. (See Chapter 3)

**Myth #3: "Old equipment is fine"**
Reality: Old equipment costs you revenue, utility efficiency, and customer experience. Calculate the true cost of "saving money."

**Myth #4: "Just copy what competitors do"**
Reality: Innovate or stagnate. The best operators differentiate.

> **LARRY'S INSIGHT:** "In 50 years, I've never seen a successful laundromat built on luck. Every winner I know did their homework, built their systems, and stuck to their numbers. The ones who failed? They chased emotions instead of data."

## Chapter 2: The Truth Behind the Spin — Competitive Intelligence

Let's be real: laundromats are not "easy money."

They're **steady money** if you design them right.  
They're **freedom** if you manage them like a business, not a hobby.  
And they're **hell** if you skip due diligence.

### Why First-Time Buyers Fail

There's a reason so many first-time buyers fail — they chase revenue and ignore rent. They look at the quarters, not the cracks in the lease.

You're buying a living, breathing ecosystem. Every laundromat has **four main organs:**

1. **Lease** — Bad lease = slow death
2. **Equipment** — Wrong mix = lost revenue
3. **Utilities** — Uncontrolled costs = no profit
4. **Community** — Wrong fit = empty machines

If one of those fails, the body starts to die.

### The Core Principle

Every chapter in this book comes back to one core principle:

> **Form over function. Structure over emotion. Design over guesswork.**

When you treat your laundromat like a living system — not a side hustle — it rewards you with freedom, recurring revenue, and longevity.

### Reality Check: The Numbers

**Typical Laundromat Economics:**
- **Revenue:** $100K-$500K annually (varies by size)
- **Net Margin:** 30-40% (well-run stores)
- **Payback Period:** 4-7 years
- **Time to Breakeven:** 18-36 months
- **Active Management:** 10-20 hours/week initially

This isn't passive income. It's **systematized** income.

### Competitive Intelligence Framework

**Step 1: Map Your Competition**
- Visit every laundromat within 3 miles
- Note: prices, hours, equipment age, cleanliness, amenities
- Use CLEANBI to score each location

**Step 2: Identify Gaps**
- What services are missing?
- What hours are underserved?

**Step 3: Position Strategically**
- Don't compete on price alone
- Compete on experience, convenience, or specialization

<a name="chapter-3"></a>
## Chapter 3: Location Is Law — Demographics and CLEANBI Scoring

You can fix equipment. You can renegotiate rent.  
**But you can't fix a bad location.**

If you ignore this law, the business gods will punish you every month with low turns and high regret.

### Location determines 80% of your success.

**What to Look For:**

- **Density:** At least 1,500 renter-occupied households within one mile
- **Visibility:** Easy in-and-out access from a main road
- **Parking:** 1.5 spaces per washer — non-negotiable
- **Competition:** Two or fewer competing stores in a one-mile radius
- **Demographics:** Median household income between $30K–$65K is the laundromat sweet spot

### The C.L.E.A.N. Principle

The Kremers doctrine uses **C.L.E.A.N.** for every potential location:

**C — Community Fit:** Who are you serving?  
**L — Lease Logic:** Are the terms sustainable?  
**E — Equipment Mix:** Is it balanced for volume?  
**A — Accessibility:** Parking, traffic flow, safety  
**N — Numbers:** Rent ratio, utilities, EBITDA

Run these checks before you ever sign a lease.

### CLEANBI Scoring System

CLEANBI is our proprietary scoring system that analyzes locations across multiple weighted factors:

**Scoring Factors:**
- Rent Ratio (15% weight)
- Turn Count/TPD (20% weight)
- Parking Capacity (10% weight)
- Income Fit (15% weight)
- Utility Load (10% weight)
- Competition Radius (15% weight)
- Demographic Match (10% weight)
- EBITDA Margin (15% weight)

**Score Grades:**
- **A (85-100):** Excellent — Top 5% of locations, invest with confidence
- **B (70-84):** Good — Above average, solid investment
- **C (55-69):** Fair — Workable with improvements
- **Needs Work (<55):** High risk — Avoid or negotiate deep discount

### Red Flags to Avoid

**Immediate Disqualifiers:**
- Less than 1,000 renters within 1 mile
- Rent exceeds 25% of projected revenue
- No parking or street parking only
- High crime area (check police reports)
- 3+ competitors within 1 mile
- Declining neighborhood (check census trends)

**Warning Signs (Proceed with Caution):**
- Lease term under 5 years
- Landlord resistance to exclusivity clause
- Recent new construction permits nearby
- Changing demographics (gentrification)
- Seasonal population fluctuations

> **LARRY'S INSIGHT:** "I've evaluated over 2,000 laundromats in my career. The single biggest predictor of success? Renter density. If you have 1,500+ renters within a mile, you can overcome almost any other challenge. If you don't, all the marketing in the world won't save you."

<a name="chapter-4"></a>
## Chapter 4: Lease or Lose — Negotiation and Trap Avoidance

Bad leases ruin good businesses.  
A perfect location with a bad lease is still a ticking time bomb.

### Lease Essentials

Here's what I've learned — mostly the hard way:

**Essential Lease Terms:**

1. **Term:** Minimum 10 years with two 5-year options
2. **Rent Ratio:** Keep total rent under 25% of gross revenue
3. **Triple Net:** Clarify who pays property tax, insurance, and maintenance
4. **HVAC Clause:** Make sure you're not paying for the landlord's equipment
5. **Exclusivity Clause:** Prevent another laundromat from opening nearby
6. **ADA and Utility Upgrades:** Confirm who covers compliance costs

### Critical Lease Protections

**YOU Must Have:**
- Right of first refusal if building sells
- Sublease rights (for potential sale)
- 60-day notice for rent increases
- Clear utility responsibility breakdown
- Landlord approval for equipment liens
- Assignment rights for business transfer

**Negotiation Leverage Points:**
- Offer longer lease for lower base rent
- Request TI (tenant improvement) allowance
- Negotiate first 3-6 months free for buildout
- Ask for rent abatement if repairs needed
- Get landlord to pay for grease trap installation

### The Attorney Rule

**Always bring in a commercial lease attorney before signing — not your cousin's divorce lawyer.**

A $2,000 legal fee can save you $200,000 in bad lease costs.

> **LARRY'S TRAP ALERT:** "The #1 lease mistake I see? Buyers who accept a 5-year lease because the seller says 'the landlord will renew.' Maybe. Maybe not. Get it in writing or walk away. I've seen operators lose everything when landlords refused to renew or tripled the rent."

### Common Lease Traps

1. **The Personal Guarantee Trap** — Avoid personally guaranteeing beyond the lease term
2. **The CAM Escalation Trap** — Cap Common Area Maintenance increases
3. **The Exclusivity Loophole** — "Coin laundry" exclusivity doesn't stop "laundry services"
4. **The Demolition Clause** — Can they tear down for redevelopment?
5. **The Hours Restriction** — Some leases limit 24-hour operation

## Chapter 5: Machine IQ — Brands, Specs, Diagnostics

There's no one "best" brand — only the best fit for your market and philosophy.

### Equipment Brand Breakdown

**SPEED QUEEN / ALLIANCE (Premium Tier)**
- Pros: 20-25 year lifespan, best-in-class warranty, highest resale value
- Cons: 40-50% higher upfront cost
- Best For: High-volume stores, long-term investors
- Price Range: $5,500-$8,000 (front load)

**DEXTER LAUNDRY (American Workhorse)**
- Pros: Built like tanks, simple controls, Dexter Financial makes funding turnkey, Dexter Live cloud monitoring
- Cons: Mid-range price point, occasional repair delays
- Best For: Commercial operators prioritizing reliability
- Price Range: $4,500-$6,500 (front load)

**CONTINENTAL GIRBAU (Efficiency Leader)**
- Pros: Front-load champions, lowest water usage (18 gal), solid spin speeds
- Cons: Higher upfront cost
- Best For: Eco-conscious operations, premium markets
- Price Range: $12,000-$22,000 (large capacity)

**ELECTROLUX / WASCOMAT (European Engineering)**
- Pros: Precision-engineered, upscale appearance
- Cons: Higher parts costs, smaller dealer network
- Best For: Premium positioning, high-end neighborhoods
- Price Range: $6,000-$9,000

**HUEBSCH (Value Play)**
- Pros: Essentially Speed Queen under different badge, parts compatible
- Cons: Less brand recognition
- Best For: Budget-conscious buyers wanting quality
- Price Range: $4,800-$7,200

**MAYTAG COMMERCIAL (Reliable Entry)**
- Pros: Reliable, familiar brand
- Cons: Shorter commercial life
- Best For: Starters, smaller stores
- Price Range: $2,800-$3,500

### Equipment Mix 101

**The Golden Ratios:**
- Large washers (60–80 lb): anchor your revenue
- Medium washers (40–60 lb): most used machines
- Small washers (20–30 lb): convenience and "quick wash"
- **Dryers: 1.5x total washer poundage capacity**

Balance is everything.

### Doctrine Tip

> Don't fall in love with a brand — fall in love with serviceability and uptime.

> **LARRY'S INSIGHT:** "In my experience, the best-performing stores aren't always the ones with the newest equipment — they're the ones with the best-maintained equipment. A 10-year-old Speed Queen that's been serviced regularly will outperform a 3-year-old cheap brand that's been neglected. Buy quality, maintain religiously."

## Chapter 6: Design and Flow — Optimizing Layouts

The best laundromats don't happen by accident — they're engineered.

Think of your floor like an assembly line.  
Customers enter, load, wait, and fold — all without friction.

### Golden Layout Rules

1. **Entry must immediately show washers in motion** — energy attracts
2. **Restroom in sight but not central** — accessible but not focal point
3. **Folding tables by dryers** — not washers (workflow logic)
4. **Changers, soap, and seating in a visibility triangle**
5. **Avoid dead corners or bottlenecks** — every square foot earns

### The Silent Salesmen

**Lighting matters** — Bright, clean, LED lighting creates safety perception  
**Music matters** — Soft background music increases dwell time 12%  
**Clean smells matter** — Fresh scent = perceived cleanliness

These are your silent salesmen.

### Kremers Doctrine

> "Comfort equals currency." A customer who feels safe and seen will spend more and return often.

### Store Layout by Size

**SMALL STORE (1,200-1,800 sq ft):**
- Washers along perimeter walls
- Dryers in center island
- Folding tables at rear
- Changer near entry
- Restroom back corner

**MEDIUM STORE (1,800-2,500 sq ft):**
- U-shaped washer configuration
- Double dryer island
- Multiple folding zones
- 2 changers (entry + center)
- Vending against wall
- TV lounge area

**LARGE STORE (2,500+ sq ft):**
- Zoned by capacity (express, standard, bulk)
- Multi-island dryer layout
- Dedicated fold room
- Wash-dry-fold counter
- Kids play area
- Multiple seating clusters

> **LARRY'S INSIGHT:** "I designed my first store in 1974 with one principle: watch the customers. Where do they naturally walk? Where do they look for things? Where do they get frustrated? After 135+ store designs, I've learned that the best layouts are the ones you don't notice — because everything just makes sense."

## Chapter 8: Operations Mastery — Efficiency, POS, Maintenance

You make money when machines run — not when they rest.

### Key Metrics to Track

**TPD (Turns Per Day)**
- Formula: Total cycles ÷ Number of machines ÷ Operating days
- Target: 4-6 turns minimum
- Urban benchmark: 4.5 TPD
- Rural benchmark: 4.0 TPD

**EBITDA**
- Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, Amortization
- Target margin: >20%
- Formula: Revenue - (Utilities + Labor + Rent + Maintenance)

**DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio)**
- Formula: NOI ÷ (Monthly Payment × 12)
- Target: >1.25 = Safe
- <1.0 = Danger zone

### Efficiency Checklist

- **Track turns per day (TPD)** — Aim for 4–6 minimum
- **Keep dryers hot and vents clean** — lint kills profits
- **Replace belts, seals, and bearings on schedule** — not when they fail
- **Control utilities:**
  - Water and sewer costs are your silent killer
  - Invest in high-efficiency washers and softeners
  - Monitor usage with smart meters

### Maintenance Discipline

**Create a logbook for every machine.**

Track issues, repairs, and replacements like an aircraft mechanic.

**Essential Metrics:**
- Downtime hours per machine per month
- Repair cost per machine annually
- Turns per machine per day
- Revenue per machine per month

Clean means credibility — inside and out.

## Chapter 9: Staffing and Systems — Automation to Attendants

You can't automate people — but you can build systems that keep them accountable.

Even if you're self-service only, you'll eventually deal with attendants, cleaners, or maintenance techs.

### Attendant Structure

**Staffing Ratios:**
- 1 attendant per 1,500 sq ft during peak hours
- Clear written task lists: cleaning, restocking, wiping machines, customer check-ins
- End-of-shift checklist signed and dated
- Bonus system for Google reviews and uptime

### Leadership Mindset

Your job isn't to do everything — it's to design a system where everything gets done without you.

### Doctrine Reminder

> Leadership is architecture. You don't micromanage bolts — you build blueprints that hold.

### Essential Systems

**Opening Checklist (30 min):**
1. Unlock and lights on
2. Test all changers
3. Verify all machines operational
4. Restock soap/vend items
5. Restroom check and clean
6. Sweep main aisles
7. Turn on music/TV

**Closing Checklist (45 min):**
1. Final walk-through (check for items left)
2. Empty trash and clean restrooms
3. Wipe down all folding tables
4. Sweep and mop floors
5. Check all machines off/locked
6. Count cash and complete deposit
7. Lock up and arm security

> Authenticity sells. Nobody cares about "brand tone." They care about clean clothes and fair prices.

- Free dry Tuesdays (fills slow days)

- Senior discount mornings

**Waste of Money:**
- Radio ads (0.2% conversion)
- Billboard (unless right on site)
- Coupon mailers (1% redemption)

## Chapter 12: Scaling Smart — Multi-Store and Valuations

Growth isn't about adding stores. It's about multiplying systems.

### The 3-Store Rule

Before expanding, you should:

1. Run your first store hands-off for 3 months
2. Have at least one trained manager who can replicate your systems
3. Document every process (opening, closing, cash-out, repairs)

Then — and only then — think about a second store.

### Multi-Store Metrics Dashboard

**Track Across All Locations:**
- Revenue per square foot
- Turns per machine per day
- Utility cost as % of revenue
- Labor cost as % of revenue
- Net margin by location
- Customer acquisition cost
- Lifetime customer value

### Valuation Methodologies

**EBITDA Multiple Method:**
- 2.5-4x annual EBITDA (well-documented stores)
- 1.5-2.5x EBITDA (distressed or poor records)
- 4-6x EBITDA (premium locations, modern equipment)

**Value Multipliers:**
- Long lease with options (+20%)
- Under-market rent (+15%)
- New equipment (<3 years) (+25%)
- Wash-dry-fold operation (+30%)
- Documented systems/SOPs (+15%)

### Scaling Wisdom

> You don't grow by adding chaos. You grow by cloning clarity.

17. Interview current customers
18. Check Google reviews/complaints
19. Assess parking adequacy
20. Verify zoning compliance

Franchise laundromats promise brand recognition, systems, and support. Here's the reality:

- Mandatory equipment purchases (marked up)

**What You Get (Often):**
- A brand nobody recognizes anyway
- Systems you could build yourself
- Restrictions on hours, pricing, and services

**Going Solo Means:**
- 100% of profit stays with you
- Freedom to pivot, innovate, adapt
- Choose your own vendors
- Set your own prices
- Build YOUR brand equity

> **LARRY'S INSIGHT:** "In 50 years, I've never seen a franchise laundromat outperform a well-run independent in the same market. The franchise fees eat your margin, and the brand recognition isn't worth what they claim. Build your own systems, keep your money."
